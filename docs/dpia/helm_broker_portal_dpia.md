# Helm Broker Portal — DPIA working draft

Status: prototype assessment for review. Owner, controller, DPO, legal reviewer and sign-off dates are not yet assigned. No assessment in this document authorises real consumer processing. This version describes the implementation and the decisions still needed before release. Use fictional records until the named decision makers approve the processing, contracts, safeguards and remaining risks.

## Processing and accountability

The proposed service supports private medical insurance advisers. A separate consumer portal collects requests; this repository supplies a broker workspace and an integration boundary. Separating the websites simplifies development, but does not separate responsibilities for personal information. The organisation must identify which entity determines purposes and means, which suppliers act on instructions, and whether any insurance partner acts as an independent controller. Record the legal entities and contact details in a processing inventory before connecting the portals.

An adviser can view a request, review the demands and needs record, authorise a quotation request, compare policies and prepare a suitability pack. An authorised adviser signs an exact pack after authentication. Learning evaluates recorded choices and proposes bounded weight changes. Compliance and a separate lead broker must approve a challenger before shadow evaluation and again before promotion. This does not make advice solely automated. Human review must remain meaningful: reviewers need time, training, usable evidence and authority to reject the result.

The local prototype stores fictional names, postcode districts, requested coverage, priorities, budgets, medical statements, vulnerability indicators, policy quotations, preferences and audit records. Staff accounts contain identity, role, authentication material and device metadata. Authentication challenges and customer snapshots use application encryption. Operational metadata includes event types, times, actor identifiers, prompt hashes, model names and token counts. The inventory must distinguish each category, purpose, recipient, retention rule and access permission rather than treating all database fields as one dataset.

## Data flow and recipients

A1 creates structured requirements and source references. The local prototype executes a two-agent Crew with a deterministic local model fixture; it makes no paid or external model call. Medical ambiguity is checked before the Crew runs, and medical statements are not included in its context. Statements are preserved as supplied and are not interpreted as diagnoses or coverage decisions. The knowledge base supplies procedural guidance, not clinical advice. Document extraction is bounded; scanned documents without a text layer require human intervention because production OCR is not integrated. A low-confidence or missing fact must be corrected at source or explicitly reviewed rather than guessed.

A2 builds a minimised request for fictional partners. It includes the requirements and postcode district and can include a category flag after a human release decision. It does not send the stored verbatim medical statement. The simulator returns quotations and exception scenarios. The MockCore adapter demonstrates an authenticated service boundary and idempotent publication. Real partner routes, partner permissions, terms of business, insurer authorisation checks, transport, recipient handling and medical underwriting procedures all require separate verification before the adapter handles real information.

A3 computes policy scores in code. A4 prepares an editable suitability pack. The optional language model gateway is disabled by default. If enabled, it applies an allow-list, regional configuration, redaction and a token budget before any provider call. A5 uses criterion utilities and explicit adviser preferences; disputed selections are excluded from fitting and enter a judgment notebook. Avoid sending identity or free medical text to learning, metrics, traces or error monitoring. Each future recipient must be added to the data flow diagram and supplier register before integration.

## Necessity and lawful processing decisions

The organisation must document the purpose and necessity of every processing activity. A request for insurance, a regulatory record, staff security monitoring and statistical improvement may have different purposes and lawful bases. Neither a user interface consent checkbox nor the fact that a customer requested a quotation establishes every required legal basis. Determine an Article 6 basis and a separate Article 9 condition for special category processing, together with any applicable domestic conditions and policy documents. Counsel and the DPO must verify the proposed analysis against the actual business model.

The ICO explains the relationship between these bases and conditions in its [special category guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/). That page identifies guidance under review following legislative changes. The release reviewer must check current guidance and record the version consulted. This draft deliberately does not select a condition on the organisation's behalf. Document any use of explicit consent, how it is obtained, how withdrawal is handled and the effect on necessary insurance records. Do not assume a health-care condition applies merely because insurance concerns health.

Coverage preferences, household cover type, budget and hospital requirements help compare policies. Exact addresses, full dates of birth, national health identifiers and diagnoses are unnecessary for the deterministic ranking inputs implemented here. Postcode district is less precise than an address but can still identify someone when combined with other facts. Challenge each requested field and justify its presence. A minimum-field request must still be accurate enough for an insurer to make the relevant quotation or underwriting decision; minimisation must not create misleading advice.

## Risk method and residual decisions

The review team should score likelihood and severity separately for each risk, identify affected people and define an acceptance owner. Consider financial loss, distress, discrimination, loss of confidentiality, delayed care-related insurance arrangements and inability to obtain suitable cover. A control's existence does not establish its effectiveness. Link each mitigation to a test, operational procedure or independently reviewed contract. Record residual risk after safeguards, planned actions, due dates and the person accepting that risk. The implementation ledger must remain separate from the legal approval record.

Start with an unauthorised user reading a medical statement. A compromised session, incorrect role, overly broad export or forgotten debug endpoint could disclose sensitive information. The prototype uses encrypted snapshots, role checks and redaction by default, including evidence exports. Explicit reveal is limited to designated roles. Encryption does not protect information after an authorised application decrypts it. Before release, independently test every response path, backup, support workflow, administrative access and emergency-access procedure. Review whether role permissions need a case-assignment boundary in addition to staff roles.

Another risk is misleading advice caused by incorrect source facts, insurer mappings or arithmetic. The code checks premium arithmetic, missing requirements, versioned documents, selections and human approval. Criterion values remain ordinal representations of supplier terms, not a complete description of cover. Obtain and version partner mapping specifications. Train advisers to inspect exclusions and unmet needs rather than relying on a score. Retain the exact inputs, algorithm version, ranking and pack so a subsequent reviewer can reconstruct the decision. Independent calibration and product governance review remain required.

## Models, redaction and transfers

A provider receiving identifiable medical text could create a disclosure or international transfer risk. The default path makes no provider call. The optional gateway removes known identifiers, recognisable identifier patterns and stored medical canaries, then keeps only hashed prompt metadata in the database. Its placeholder mapping is encrypted in Redis with a one-hour expiry. Pattern matching is imperfect, particularly for unusual names, misspellings, another language or indirect identification through context. Redacted information must be treated as potentially personal information unless a competent assessment establishes otherwise.

Before enabling a provider, verify the actual service region, subprocessors, support access locations, retention, training exclusions, deletion rights and incident commitments. A configuration value saying UK or EU does not prove where processing occurs. Obtain contractual and technical evidence from the supplier. Assess transfer mechanisms and any additional measures with counsel; do not infer adequacy from a supplier's headquarters or marketing description. Keep raw prompts out of Langfuse, OpenTelemetry and vendor dashboards. The current prototype records minimal LLM-call metadata locally; external model observability is not a completed release control.

Prompt injection is another source of risk. A partner message or document can contain instructions that appear authoritative. The deterministic classifier treats such content as data, returns a constrained classification and flags supported attack families. Typed schemas reject invalid premium fields. No partner text can grant approval permissions. The generated red-team cases test known instruction, role-prefix, SQL-shaped, HTML and Unicode attacks; they do not prove immunity to all attacks. Future model-assisted extraction requires an adversarial evaluation on realistic documents and must not acquire publication tools.

## Authentication and staff access

The prototype implements server-verified passkeys, TOTP, one-use recovery codes, session rotation and replay detection. Session secrets stay in HttpOnly cookies. Authentication challenges are short-lived and consumed once. Device revocation also revokes refresh access. Advice approval requires a fresh, session-bound step-up challenge and a registered adviser identifier. The local demonstration permits a password fallback when an authenticator is not enrolled. A production enrollment policy, recovery verification process, help-desk procedure and mandatory MFA enforcement are outstanding organisational decisions and release work.

Review joiners, movers and leavers. Record who can assign adviser and compliance roles, designate a lead broker, verify authorisation and revoke access. An administrator should not gain advice authority simply by being an administrator. Separate development credentials from production identities. Confirm inactivity, absolute session lifetime, device visibility, cookie domain, TLS termination and secure-cookie settings. Rate limiting must be shared across instances and fail safely when Redis is unavailable. The local prototype uses an opaque session reference rather than the blueprint's access JWT; document and review that deliberate architecture choice.

## Evidence, retention and rights

The application stores append-only snapshots, approval records, publication evidence and a chained activity log. Database triggers reject direct mutation of evidence records. Daily verification computes an audit root and reports failures. The local root is database-held; independently managed write-once anchoring and external alert delivery are deployment tasks. A single database operator with sufficiently broad privileges remains a threat to both evidence and its local verification records. Use separated privileges, monitored access, encrypted backups and an external anchor before relying on the chain as independent tamper evidence.

The requested seven-year evidential retention period is a proposal requiring review, not an implemented or universally applicable rule. Define different retention periods for enquiries, abandoned cases, medical source statements, regulatory advice evidence, authentication logs, redaction mappings and backups. Confirm legal holds, complaint records and disposal exceptions. The prototype intentionally does not automatically delete evidential records. Implement and test an approved disposal process, including backup expiry and supplier deletion, before live release. Avoid promising erasure that conflicts with a documented, applicable record-keeping obligation.

Define how people receive clear notices and exercise their rights. Establish identity verification for access requests, a search process across both portals and suppliers, and secure delivery of an appropriate response. Consider information about other household members and third-party medical statements when preparing a disclosure. Provide a way to correct inaccurate facts while retaining an appropriate record of the original advice and correction. A new DNR version must invalidate its earlier sign-off and any dependent draft. Do not silently overwrite a signed record to make an export look current.

## Complaints and vulnerable customers

Vulnerability indicators should trigger human-led handling and careful review, not a negative ranking adjustment. Keyword screening in the prototype is intentionally limited and can miss context or produce false positives. Advisers must be able to record a concern that the screen missed and challenge an incorrect flag. Decide who can see vulnerability details, what accommodations are offered and how staff avoid unnecessary repetition of sensitive information. Validate terminology and workflows with people who understand vulnerable customer handling before customer research uses real experiences.

A complaint hold blocks agent work. The prototype assigns a compliance owner and records a reminder date, but that reminder is not a determination of the applicable complaint deadline. The operating procedure must identify the first indication of dissatisfaction, its receipt date, the relevant response requirements, escalation, final-response wording and any referral rights. Compliance must validate the timetable and own any external response. Internal closure requires a recorded resolution by the designated owner. Do not send automated legal or regulatory correspondence solely because a timer has elapsed.

## Operational security and incident response

Local Docker credentials and demonstration accounts are intentionally fictional. Before release, replace defaults with independently generated secrets, use a managed secret store and establish key rotation and recovery. Test restore procedures using a protected backup and a preserved encryption key. A backup without the key may be unusable; a backup beside an unprotected key may defeat the encryption objective. Configure TLS on every relevant hop, restricted network access, least-privilege database accounts, patch management and supplier monitoring. Dependency audit output must be triaged, not suppressed to produce a clean badge.

Prepare an incident procedure that distinguishes a failed job, evidence corruption, account compromise and potential personal-data breach. Identify an incident lead, deputy, technical responders and DPO contact. Set internal reporting deadlines and require the responsible reviewers to assess any notification duties against current law and the facts. Preserve evidence, minimise further exposure and record decisions. Do not place raw customer records in chat, alert messages or public tickets. Exercise the procedure using fictional canaries and verify that the team can revoke sessions, pause agents and recover service without losing approvals.

## Consultation and approval record

Consult the DPO, compliance owner, security lead, adviser lead, product owner and procurement or legal reviewers. Identify appropriate consumer consultation and accessibility research. Complete supplier due diligence and the processing inventory before enabling a real integration. If residual risk remains high, obtain the required assessment and advice rather than treating this draft as permission to proceed. Record each reviewer, findings, required actions, evidence and date. Any approval must state the exact deployment, suppliers and purposes covered, including whether optional language-model processing is permitted.

Revisit this assessment when a provider, insurer route, source document type, model, ranking criterion, medical data flow, retention schedule or intended user population changes. Review it after significant incidents or complaints and on a scheduled basis owned by the DPO. Link approved changes to the model card, launch checklist, control register and release evidence. The prototype offers concrete controls and tests for that review; it does not replace accountable human decisions or establish that a production deployment is compliant.
